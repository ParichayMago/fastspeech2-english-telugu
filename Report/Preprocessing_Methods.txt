Preprocessing methods and limitations - FastSpeech 2 assignment

English speaker labels are ESTIMATED acoustic clusters
-----------------------------------------------------
Original Svarah speaker IDs are absent from the official downloaded snapshot and
its earliest metadata-only upload. The historical archive returns 403 and the
publisher's issue tracker records missing speaker IDs. Demographic fields produce
115 distinct profiles for 117 documented speakers; they are not used as identity.

The user authorized estimated acoustic labels on 3 November 2026. Selected clips
are grouped by filename recording roots, with whole recording groups kept within
one split. Up to 12 seconds of selected speech from each recording are concatenated
for a pretrained SpeechBrain ECAPA-TDNN speaker embedding. The public pretrained
model is speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb, revision
0f99f2d0ebe89ac095bcc5903c4dd8f72b367286, under Apache-2.0. SpeechBrain version 1.0.3
runs inference on mono 16 kHz waveforms. No model was fitted to this assignment's
validation audio during embedding extraction.

Average-linkage cosine clustering uses only training recordings with at least
3 seconds of usable speech and a heuristic distance threshold of 0.30. This
produced 254 ESTIMATED clusters from 1,041 training recordings. These are NOT
254 verified individuals, NOT the publisher's original IDs, and NOT proof that
the corpus contains 254 speakers. A voice can be fragmented into multiple labels,
and different voices can be merged. Cluster count is not forced to the reported
117 speakers. Gender and demographics are not clustering inputs.

Short training recordings and validation recordings are assigned to frozen
training centroids. All 3,656 selected English clips receive labels en_est_XXXX.
Original speaker IDs remain null. Manifests explicitly include
speaker_id_estimated=true and speaker_id_provenance, plus review scores. Low
cosine similarity (<0.50), low centroid margin (<0.05), or less than 3 seconds of
speech triggers review. This flags 213/1,253 training recording groups and 94/302
validation groups. Scores are heuristic review aids, not calibrated probabilities
or identity-verification accuracy. No human listening validation is claimed.

Frozen data selections
----------------------
The split seed is 20261102. English has 3,039 training clips (~5 hours) and 617
validation clips (~1 hour). Telugu uses the source's male-labelled recording group:
2,153 training clips (~5 hours), 428 validation clips (~1 hour), source label te_male.
The Telugu choice is documented subsampling, consistent with the assignment's
language/duration requirements; it is not an acoustic identity estimate.

Speaker labeling did not change any split membership. Connected components of
normalized repeated transcripts, exact audio hashes and same-recording English
chunks do not cross splits. Original source WAV bytes and transcripts are backed
up locally. Output exports were checked against source SHA-256 hashes and headers.
These are utterance/recording-held-out TTS splits, not a speaker-disjoint benchmark.

Text and pronunciations
-----------------------
Raw transcripts are retained. English uses the reference implementation's
english_cleaners (including abbreviation and number expansion), followed by
word tokenization and g2p-en 2.1.0 ARPAbet pronunciations. The resulting lexicon
contains 6,470 words. Telugu uses NFC, num2words 0.5.14 Telugu expansions and
Unicode letter/mark word tokenization. Phonemizer 3.3.0 with eSpeak-NG's Telugu
backend generates pronunciations for 13,848 words. Stress markers are removed;
IPA phones map deterministically to pure lowercase alphabetic telph* identifiers, recorded in
ipa-to-phone.json and the source-controlled phone inventory. Existing English
symbol IDs are preserved by appending Telugu symbols.
An initial underscore-based encoding was shortened during MFA model export/use,
which the validation feature smoke check rejected. The replacement uses only
lowercase ASCII letters, including a bijective encoding of Unicode code points.
The Telugu aligner is rebuilt with those safe labels. Language-specific phone
inventories are checked in addition to global symbol lookup; unknown/collapsed
phones are rejected, never silently added or dropped.

Known validation text can be phonemized deterministically to cover vocabulary;
no validation audio fits a pronunciation model. Both languages have zero shared
normalized transcript strings across their selected splits after this processing.
Automatic pronunciations, number normalization and semantic transcript/audio
agreement still require listening review. Telugu test text may contain spelling,
number, ordinal and code-mixing cases needing specific evaluation later.

Waveforms and alignment
-----------------------
Native sources are 16 kHz mono English and 48 kHz mono for the selected Telugu
male subset. Corpus copies use scipy.signal.resample_poly to 22,050 Hz, PCM-16.
No waveform is trimmed to manufacture split durations. Interpolation overshoots
above .999 are scaled to .999; this affected 171 English and 37 Telugu clips.
Original audio stays unchanged. Resampled split durations remain approximately 5/1
hours. Resampling adds at most rounding-scale duration differences per utterance.

MFA 3.3.9 runs in a separate conda-forge environment, with SQLite and dedicated
per-language scratch directories. English uses the pretrained english_us_arpa
acoustic model and the generated ARPAbet lexicon. Speaker adaptation is disabled
because English identity labels are estimated. Telugu uses its generated lexicon
and trains an acoustic aligner on the Telugu TRAINING corpus only: monophone
(1,000-utterance subset, 30 iterations, 1,000 Gaussians), then triphone (all training
utterances, 25 iterations, 1,500 leaves, 5,000 Gaussians). Its frozen aligner is then
used on the validation corpus. This acoustic aligner is not a trained FastSpeech 2
TTS model. Actual completion, failures and durations belong in machine-readable
reports; do not interpret this method description alone as proof of completion.

FastSpeech 2 targets
--------------------
Phone TextGrid intervals provide duration supervision. Real mel spectrograms have
80 bins, 1,024-sample FFT/window, 256-sample hop at 22,050 Hz, with 0-8,000 Hz mel
limits. WORLD DIO plus StoneMask produces F0; pitch and energy are averaged at the
phone level. Leading/trailing silence is trimmed using alignment intervals.
Every accepted example must have finite arrays, known phone IDs, matching phone
feature lengths, and sum(phone durations) equal to mel frame count. Failed
alignment, unusable pitch and inconsistent arrays are logged and excluded, rather
than receiving fabricated/equal durations or synthetic features.

Pitch/energy mean and standard deviation fit training examples only (per-example
IQR outlier filtering for fitting); validation uses frozen training statistics.
Phone min/max bins also derive from training targets. Validation examples whose
estimated label has no surviving training examples are excluded and logged. Actual
accepted clip counts and feature hours can be smaller than source selections;
reports must state both. Train/validation membership is never randomly reshuffled
by the reference preprocessor.

Measured completion, 3 November 2026
-----------------------------------
English: 3,037 training examples and 617 validation examples accepted. Two source
training examples are excluded for unusable pitch/alignment. Effective aligned
feature duration is 3.90162 / 0.79944 hours after boundary trimming, from source
selections of approximately 5 / 1 hours. Model conditioning uses 254 estimated
acoustic labels.

Telugu: all 2,153 training and 428 validation examples accepted after the phone
encoding correction. Effective aligned feature duration is 3.70511 / 0.74613 hours
after boundary trimming, from source selections of approximately 5 / 1 hours.
The model uses the single male-labelled source group.

Both languages pass small real-data CUDA forward/backward/optimizer checks with
finite training and validation loss and confirmed weight updates. These are
software compatibility checks on randomly initialized FastSpeech 2 models, not
converged training, final checkpoints, or a speech-quality evaluation. Both small
feature smoke checks cover training and validation examples. Independent saved-
array checks pass for both languages, including train-only normalization.

Raw selection hours and effective aligned hours must both be reported. The current
preprocessing does not replace trimmed boundary regions with artificial targets
or reselect data to conceal the reduction in effective hours.
