FastSpeech 2
English and Telugu
Implementation, training and test-sentence analysis
Parichay Mago | Technical report
Abstract
This work develops two independent FastSpeech 2 text-to-speech systems for English and Telugu using
Svarah and IndicTTS Telugu. We began by examining the reference architecture, checking dataset
access and transcript quality, and selecting approximately five hours of training audio and one hour of
validation audio per language. We then implemented language-specific text processing, phoneme
conversion, forced alignment and acoustic-feature extraction. Separate acoustic models predict mel
spectrograms, which a pretrained HiFi-GAN converts into speech [1-5]. The experiments investigate
training convergence, alignment quality and English prosody through baseline training, feature
refinement and targeted fine-tuning. Evaluation covers all ten assignment sentences and their predicted
duration, pitch and energy trajectories.
Implementation overview
The first implementation stage established the data pipeline. We checked usable audio duration, built
reproducible train/validation splits and handled speaker conditioning. English uses estimated acoustic
speaker labels. Telugu uses a pronunciation lexicon and a stable phone mapping compatible with the
reference implementation.
We trained separate English and Telugu models using aligned duration, pitch and energy targets. We
reviewed the alignment, updated feature extraction to preserve internal silence, rebuilt both feature sets
and trained both models from scratch.
The English experiments continued with low-rate fine-tuning and a model extension with word-boundary
and punctuation embeddings, plus predictors for pause presence and duration. Finally, we selected
checkpoints using complete validation evaluations, generated the required test audios and analysed
their variance predictions alongside the loss curves.
The report follows an introduction-method-experiments-discussion structure, informed by the Seed-TTS technical report [11].
Methods and data appear on pp. 2-5, experimental results on pp. 6-9, sentence-level analysis on pp. 10-19, and discussion,
reproducibility, software verification, speaker coverage and references on pp. 20-26.

1. Introduction and dataset preparation
Adapting a text-to-speech system to two languages requires a consistent path from written text to
pronunciation, acoustic timing and waveform generation. We adapted speaker conditioning for English,
phoneme representation for Telugu and alignment-based supervision for both. The objective was to build
and evaluate two separate systems from the reference FastSpeech 2 implementation [1,2].
The assignment specifies two separate models, approximately five hours of training audio and one hour
of validation per language, the exact five test sentences per language, loss curves, and
duration/F0/energy analysis for every test. E2a/E2b and T2a/T2b count separately, giving ten required
audios and thirty variance figures.

2. Method: architecture and language support
Phones + FFT Variance FFT + HiFi-GAN
position encoder adaptor postnet waveform
Separate acoustic models. Speaker conditioning; English adds text-derived boundary embeddings and pause heads.
The reference variant encodes phones with feed-forward Transformer blocks, predicts duration, pitch and
energy, length-regulates the sequence, and decodes mel spectrograms. A convolutional postnet refines
mels. This project uses direct phoneme-level F0 and an external HiFi-GAN [1-3].
Component Executed configuration
Encoder / decoder 4 / 6 FFT blocks; 2 attention heads; hidden size 256
FFT feed-forward Filter size 1024; kernel sizes 9 and 1; dropout 0.2
Variance predictors Filter size 256; kernel 3; dropout 0.5
Variance embeddings 256 linearly quantized bins each for pitch and energy
Length and speakers Maximum sequence 2048; English 254 estimated labels; Telugu 1 label
Acoustic targets 80-bin log mels; 22,050 Hz; FFT/window 1,024; hop 256; 0-8,000 Hz
Waveform synthesis Frozen universal HiFi-GAN; mono PCM16 output; no vocoder retraining
English learned-pause extension
Each word has a boundary slot, including words with zero measured pause. A seven-entry boundary
embedding conditions the encoder; two new variance predictors estimate pause presence and positive
pause length. Punctuation categories distinguish unpunctuated, comma, period, question, exclamation
and clause boundaries. The parent acoustic weights are loaded exactly; new heads receive a larger
learning rate. The existing pitch and energy heads also receive the new encoder context.
At inference a sigmoid threshold of 0.5 selects a positive pause. The predicted log duration becomes
rounded frames, and the length regulator generates the acoustic span. Terminal boundaries supply
sentence context and receive zero frames. The cap of 354 frames is the training-observed maximum.
Lexical durations have a one-frame floor.
Telugu adaptation
The Telugu path uses a bijective IPA-to-ASCII phone map, a language-specific pronunciation lexicon and
aligner, its own statistics and checkpoint, and the base variance adaptor. The acoustic models and
optimizers are separate, with a shared frozen vocoder.

2.1 Preprocessing and alignment
Text, audio and alignment
English normalizes text through the reference cleaners and g2p-en ARPAbet conversion. Telugu uses
NFC, number verbalization and eSpeak-NG Telugu pronunciations, mapped to stable lowercase telph
identifiers. English training pronunciations cover 6,470 words; Telugu covers 13,848. English symbol IDs
remain stable when Telugu symbols are appended [7,8].
Original English source is 16 kHz mono; the selected Telugu source is 48 kHz mono. scipy resample_poly
creates 22,050 Hz PCM16 copies. Original WAVs and raw transcripts remain unchanged. MFA 3.3.9
supplies alignments: English uses english_us_arpa with speaker adaptation disabled; Telugu fits an
acoustic aligner using training audio only, then freezes it for validation. Telugu alignment is not the TTS
model itself [6].
Internal silence in aligned targets
The gap-fixed-v2 feature extractor represents internal silence with explicit tokens, preserves the full
cropped timeline and enforces sum(duration frames)=mel length. Both language models were trained
from scratch on these features.

Feature extraction and normalization
WORLD DIO plus StoneMask estimates F0. The extractor averages interpolated F0 and frame energy over
phones. Spectral energy is the linear L2 norm. Pitch and energy means, standard deviations and bins are
fitted on training features using per-example IQR filtering. Validation uses frozen parameters. The
extractor crops edge silence, retains internal silence and verifies that arrays are finite, use known phones
and have consistent lengths.
The English pause pilot uses all 3,654 clips from the aligned feature set. Each internal word boundary
receives its measured duration in frames, including zero where the alignment shows no silence. The pilot
preserves lexical phone durations, pitch and energy, source mel bytes, split membership, speaker
mapping and normalization statistics.
Supporting records are Report/Alignment_Gap_Fix.txt, English/artifacts/Preprocessing/gap-fixed-v2/ and
English/artifacts/Preprocessing/learned-pauses-v1/. Normalization statistics, speaker centroids and the Telugu alignment
model are fitted on training data.

Executed runner arguments override the upstream batch 16 / 900k-step template. Training GPUs were
RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell for the corrected paired run, A40 for the fine-tune, and RTX 3090 for the pause
pilot; PyTorch 2.8.0 with CUDA 12.8 and Python 3.10 were used. Final inference and this analysis run
locally on CPU.
Base objective: five loss terms
Lbase = Lmel + Lpostnet + Lpitch + Lenergy + Llog_duration
Mel terms use L1 error; pitch, energy and log(1+duration) use MSE. Six numbers are logged: total
followed by the five component losses. No claim of six separate component terms is intended.
Learned-pause objective: seven loss terms
Lpause = Lmel + Lpostnet + Lpitch + Lenergy + Lspeech_duration
+ 0.25 * Lpresence + 0.25 * Lpositive_pause_duration
Pause presence uses weighted BCE, with training-only positive weight 2.9192; positive pause length uses
log-duration MSE. Speech-duration loss excludes boundary slots; pitch/energy losses exclude tokens
without target frames. Eight numbers are logged including total. Because both terms and masks change,
base and pause totals are not directly comparable.
Validation interpretation
Checkpoint selection uses complete held-out evaluations. Batch losses are averaged with example-count
weights. Validation supplies target duration, pitch and energy to the mel decoder and records predictor
errors alongside mel losses. Training minibatches use dropout.

Conclusions
The system preserves the source text and reproduces all ten test outputs. It predicts varying
