Purpose
The previous punctuation frontend inserted silence and scaled durations after
splitting text. This pilot instead trains pause presence/duration inside the
model. A shared frontend places a boundary slot after EVERY word, including
words with no recorded pause. Punctuation category embeddings provide context to
the encoder, duration heads, and existing pitch/energy predictors.

Training and inference
All parent weights are loaded unchanged before training. New boundary embeddings
and two variance predictors are added. Pause-presence weighted BCE and positive
pause log-duration MSE are each weighted 0.25; the other losses retain full mel,
postnet mel, active-token pitch/energy, and lexical-phone duration supervision.
The optimizer is new Adam: parent parameters 2e-5, new pause parameters 2e-4.
Additional pilot steps start at zero. Full 617-example validation establishes a
new step-zero baseline; the eight-component total cannot be directly compared
with the parent's old six-component validation loss 2.5788.
A 90-minute / 20000-step bound and eight non-improving validations constrain the
experiment. Save latest every100 steps and validate every500; best uses complete
validation only. The disposable GPU readiness update is never used for training.

Learned inference uses one full utterance through the model and one vocoder call.
Pause durations come from the new heads and the length regulator. It inserts no
external silence, does not split sentences, applies no duration multiplier or
waveform fades, and uses no denoising. English/inference.py detects this checkpoint
format and selects the learned inference path. Local synthesis (from the assignment workspace):
.venvs/inference/bin/python English/inference.py --run-id english-learned-pauses-pilot-20261104 --text "Hello, how are you?"

Limits of the result
The data are conversational ASR recordings with estimated acoustic speaker labels,
not verified single-speaker narration. Punctuation, especially colons/exclamations,
is sparse. This pilot tests learned pause supervision and punctuation conditioning;
it does not establish natural intonation, clarity, or convergence. Existing
phoneme-level pitch/energy targets remain in use. Model quality must be judged
using the same untreated text/speaker/vocoder before and after, especially the MIT
passage. Preserve earlier models as comparisons and do not claim improvement from
loss alone. Actual launch, GPU proof, progress, budgets, and backup hashes are in
English/artifacts/Training/english-learned-pauses-pilot-20261104/.
